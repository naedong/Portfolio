Why I Built This

University life starts with courses, but the course experience is fragmented.

Students do not only need a timetable. They need to understand which lectures exist, what each lecture means, how it fits into their semester, and who else is taking the same course. UniCal was created to connect those moments into one clear experience.

The first student problem is not search. It is structure.

A student may see a long list of lectures, but that list rarely feels like a personal academic plan. Course information, calendar planning, and student conversations often live in separate places.

UniCal turns the lecture list into a starting point for the whole semester: discover a course, understand it, save it, and connect with other students around it.

Goal

Product purpose

  • Make lecture lists easier to read on mobile.
  • Show lecture time, room, professor, credits, and basic details clearly.
  • Let students save courses into a personal university calendar.
  • Create a verified student community around courses and campus life.
User Pain

The university experience is split across systems that do not feel connected.

Lecture lists, course details, personal schedules, and student conversations are usually separated. UniCal focuses on the gap between official academic information and the real student experience.

01

Lecture lists feel heavy

Course catalogues can be difficult to scan quickly. Students need a cleaner way to compare lectures, times, rooms, and credits.

02

Planning is manual

Students often move from course pages to separate calendar apps. The academic plan is not naturally connected to the lecture list.

03

Community is disconnected

Students in the same course may not have a simple, trusted place to ask questions, share notes, or find classmates.

Market Logic

The opportunity is not “no one built student tools.” The opportunity is the missing connection.

Europe already has student services, university portals, timetable apps, housing platforms, and peer-to-peer community products. UniCal’s opportunity is narrower and clearer: connect lecture discovery, course planning, and student interaction around the actual academic experience — without depending on university contracts at the first validation stage.

Why now

Why this can work now

  • The product does not need to replace university systems.
  • It can start as an independent student-facing layer using selected, public, curated, or user-submitted lecture information.
  • Course context gives students a practical reason to return every week.
  • Official cooperation could improve data quality later, but it is not required to prove the first user demand.
Why It Has Not Been Solved Yet

The gap exists because campus tools are hard to unify, not because students do not need them.

UniCal is not claiming that no student apps exist. The stronger argument is that existing tools usually solve only one side of the problem: timetable, grades, housing, admissions chat, or general student community. The difficult part is connecting lecture data, personal planning, and course-based community in a way that is accurate, privacy-aware, and realistic to launch without depending on university contracts from day one.

01

University systems are fragmented

Each university may use different student information systems, LMS platforms, course catalogue formats, authentication flows, and internal rules. A product that works in one campus may not automatically work in the next one.

02

Course data is sensitive to quality

Lecture time, room, professor, credits, and exam-related information must be accurate. If the app shows wrong course data, students lose trust quickly. This makes automation harder than a normal content app.

03

Privacy and trust are non-negotiable

Student identity, course choices, and community activity can become personal data. In Europe, a campus app must be built with privacy, consent, data minimization, and moderation from the beginning.

Insight

This is exactly why UniCal should start independently.

Instead of trying to integrate every university immediately, UniCal should begin with one campus, one department, or curated lecture data. Partnerships are useful later, but the first goal is to prove that students want a cleaner lecture-centered planning and community experience.

Approach

Barrier-aware launch strategy

  • Start with public, curated, or user-submitted lecture data.
  • Clearly separate official information from student-generated content.
  • Add disclaimers when data is unofficial or community-maintained.
  • Use flexible student verification options only when privacy and consent are clear.
  • Keep the first version independent and small enough to test with real students.

Product Focus

UniCal is not trying to become a giant university system.

The product should stay simple: help students understand lectures, organize their academic week, and connect with other students around courses. Anything that sounds like enterprise software, paid university tools, or complex partnerships should be removed from the first version.

Keep

Lecture-centered planning

Students can browse lectures, open details, and save selected courses into their own weekly academic view.

Keep

Course-based community

Students can ask questions, share tips, and connect with classmates around courses or campus life.

Remove

Overbuilt business claims

No student premium, no enterprise dashboards, no sponsored ads, and no forced university partnership in the MVP pitch.

Execution Plan

Build small, validate with students, then improve what students actually use.

The goal is to avoid building a giant university system too early. The first version should prove that students want lecture-centered planning plus community. After validation, the product can improve based on actual student behavior instead of imaginary business models.

Phase 01

Clickable prototype

Show lecture list, course detail, save-to-calendar, and community screens.

Phase 02

Pilot dataset

Start with one small lecture dataset: one department, one semester, or manually curated sample data.

Phase 03

Student validation

Test with students and measure saves, repeats, questions, and community posts.

Phase 04

Improve and expand

Add more lectures, better filters, stronger calendar views, and useful community features based on student feedback.
